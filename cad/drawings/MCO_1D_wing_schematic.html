COVERT FLAP GEOMETRY
Position (hinge)50%c = 60mm from LE · upper surface
Free end position62%c = 74.4mm from LE (just before D-box rear wall)
Flap chord-wise width12%c = 14.4mm (within research range 8–15%c)
Number of strips3 parallel strips per wing (spanwise)
Strip span-wise widthEach strip = full wing span. All 3 identical.
Slot cutsYES — cut every 15mm along chord-to-TE axis
Slot purposeAllows each segment to respond independently to local flow — approximates feather row. Reduces spanwise stiffness so sections pop up at local separation.
Hinge methodFront 2mm of strip bonded to upper surface with E6000. Remaining 12.4mm free to deflect.
Max deployment angle~30–40° (limited by aerodynamic load in normal flight)
COVERT FLAP MATERIAL
Recommended material0.125mm (5 thou) PET film (Mylar / BoPET)
Why PET filmCorrect flex stiffness range for passive deployment at Re 4,000–10,000. Tested in wind tunnel experiments (Princeton 2023). Thin enough to deploy passively, stiff enough to return flat when AoA reduces.
Alternative0.1mm polycarbonate sheet (slightly stiffer — use if PET too floppy)
NOT recommendedRipstop nylon — too flexible, deploys at wrong AoA. Mylar tape — adhesive backing too heavy. Rigid PLA — cannot deploy passively.
Surface finishNo coating needed — smooth PET provides correct stiffness
ColourClear or light grey — minimises solar absorption affecting deployment
Cut methodLaser cut or sharp scissors/scalpel. Score slot cuts with scalpel — do NOT cut all the way to hinge edge.
Slot cut depthCut from FREE edge toward hinge. Stop 2mm before hinge line — leave connected strip for bonding.
Slot cut spacingEvery 15mm along spanwise direction
Mass per wing~0.4g (negligible)

PNAS 2024 (Sedky, Othman, Wissa — Princeton) confirmed that spatially distributed multi-strip covert flaps give up to 50% lift increase and 30% drag reduction in post-stall conditions. Single strips give up to 27% lift improvement at Re=1,000. The slotted multi-strip configuration better approximates real feather rows.

ADHESIVE BONDING — RECOMMENDED PRODUCTS AND METHODS
MEMBRANE TO RIB TAIL
AdhesiveE6000 flexible craft adhesive
WhyRemains flexible after cure — essential where membrane moves slightly under load. Strong bond to both PLA and ripstop nylon. Widely available, paintable.
MethodApply thin bead to rib tail upper and lower edges. Press membrane firmly, wipe excess. Cure 24–72hr before flying.
Surface prepLightly sand LW-PLA rib tail edge with 220 grit. Clean ripstop with IPA. No primer needed.
Bond width~2mm contact line along rib tail edge
TE hem (rod)E6000 to seal folded hem over 0.3mm CF rod after threading.
AlternativeHobbyKing UHU Por contact cement — used by RC builders for fabric-to-plastic bonds. Apply to both surfaces, let tack, press together.
CF SKIN TO RIB BODY
AdhesiveWest System 105/206 epoxy resin
WhyStructural bond. Same resin used to laminate CF skin — wet layup bonds simultaneously to LW-PLA peel-ply surface. Zero additional adhesive needed if done wet.
MethodApply CF cloth over ribs while epoxy is wet. Ribs act as formers. Resin bonds cloth to rib flange simultaneously.
Surface prepLW-PLA upper/lower surface sanded lightly (180 grit). Clean with IPA.
If dry bond neededThin CA (cyanoacrylate) + accelerator. Apply CA to rib flange, mist accelerator on CF skin back face. Press.
VG STRIP + COVERT FLAP
VG strip to wing upperE6000 along strip base — flexible, removable
Why not CA for VG stripCA is brittle — VG strip takes aerodynamic load at base, needs slight flex tolerance. E6000 absorbs micro-vibration from flapping.
VG strip prepSand bottom of strip (180 grit). Clean wing upper surface with IPA before bonding.
Covert flap hingeE6000 on front 2mm of PET strip to upper CF skin
Why E6000 for covertCreates a compliant hinge — PET film can flex from this line without peeling. CA would create a rigid brittle hinge line that cracks under repeated deployment.
CF rods (B,C,E,F) in holesThin CA (Loctite 401) — wicks into hole, fast cure, rigid bond correct here.
Main spar (A)30-min epoxy — structural, slow cure, full 5mm socket engagement.
